# Reading Reflections


## Instructions

Prepare 3 slides.

### Slide 1: Summarize the content

For a research paper this may include problem space, research questions, methods, key findings, and discussions. If it was a video or other kind of article, what was the topic, focus, key points, story, etc.

### Slide 2: Reflect on the content (Relate it to your own experiences)

*What did you think? What stood out to you? What did you learn? What was unexpected? How does this relate to the class and/or your personal experience?*

### Slide 3: Discussion Questions

Prepare two to three questions at the end of the presentation to facilitate discussion from the whole class for 5 to 10 minutes.

- Focus on questions that probe the study's implications, limitations, and connections. Here are three types of questions that work well:  
  - 1\. Questions about Implications & Application  
    - These questions explore the real-world significance of the research.  
    - Template: "Assuming these findings are correct, how should \[a specific industry, profession, or group\] change its practices?"  
    - Example: "The paper shows that four-day work weeks boost productivity. If you were a manager, what challenges might you face when trying to implement this, beyond just scheduling?"  
  - 2\. Questions about Critiques & Alternatives  
    - These questions encourage critical thinking about the study's methods and potential weaknesses.  
    - Template: "The researchers chose to study \[a specific group or context\]. How might the results have been different if they had focused on \[another group or context\] instead?"  
    - Example: "The study relied entirely on self-reported surveys to measure happiness. What are the potential flaws of this method, and what's another way they could have measured it?"  
  - 3\. Questions about Connections & Extensions  
    - These questions link the paper to course concepts or future research possibilities.  
    - Template: "How does this paper's concept of 'X' challenge or support the theory of 'Y' that we discussed last week?"  
    - Example: "The authors stopped their research here. If you had a grant to continue this work, what would be the most logical next step for a follow-up study?"  
  - 4\. What to Avoid  
    - Factual Recall Questions: Avoid questions with a single right answer found in the text.  
      - Bad Example: "What was the sample size of the study?"  
    - Yes/No Questions: Avoid questions that can be answered with a simple "yes" or "no."  
      - Bad Example: "Do you think the study was interesting?"  
    - Better Example: "What specific aspect of the study's method or findings did you find most compelling or problematic, and why?"
